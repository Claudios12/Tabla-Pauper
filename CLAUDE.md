# Project: Pauper Deck Arena

Pauper MTG deck tracker and tournament performance analyzer. Static PWA with vanilla JS/CSS and no build step. Mobile-first, Spanish UI.

## File layout

- `index.html`: the page markup only. It links the stylesheets and scripts in order.
- `css/`: one file per section (base, shell, controls, cards, deck-cards, analysis, log, league, forms, colour, views). The link order in `index.html` is the cascade order: keep it.
- `js/`: plain scripts loaded in order with `defer`, sharing globals (not ES modules, because the markup uses inline `onclick="Arena.x()"`). Order: config, state, analytics, format, league, monthly, outlook, demo, then `arena.js` (defines `Arena`) and the `arena-*.js` parts, which add methods with `Object.assign(Arena, { ... })`, then `main.js`.
- Nothing ships with the app, and nothing of any person goes in the code: no history, deck, name, closing date or league night. Whoever opens the page starts empty, and everything is theirs, saved on their own device (localStorage; there is no server). If the app needs a value from the person, it asks (never a default that happens to be the owner's). Who the person is lives in `State.profile` (`{ name, leagueDay }`, key `pauper_arena_profile`, its own test-mode key, in backups as `profile`, cleaned by `cleanProfile`), edited in the "Tu liga" dialog (`Arena.openLeagueDialog` / `saveLeague`). `League.identify(name)` tells the league who "me" is: the name as the standings screens show it, several spellings separated by commas, each judged on its own, and no name means no "me" (every place that needs it says so and offers the dialog). `State.leagueDay()` is the weekday the league plays: the one chosen, or else the one most tournaments fell on, or null (then the countdown and the risk card ask for it). The welcome card (`Arena.renderWelcome`) shows while the history is empty. Test mode is an invented league (`Demo.build()`, loaded by `State.loadDemo()` under the `pauper_test_arena_*` keys; leaving it or wiping removes them) and never touches the real keys. "Borrar mis datos" is `State.wipeAll()`. A new deck is written with its picked colours first (`deckNameWithColours`), and one that reads like a deck already there is refused.
- Tabs live in `Arena.TABS` (`arena.js`): the nav order, the `#tab=` slugs and the page titles all come from it, and each nav link carries `data-tab`. Add a tab there, not by position.
- OCR (`Arena.readStandings` in `arena-undo.js`): the picture is turned upright, enlarged when small (a screenshot shared through a chat is: the real ones are about 520 px wide, and a short side under `CONFIG.OCR_SMALL_SIDE` is brought to about `OCR_TARGET_SIDE`, at most 3 times) and scaled down only when huge (`OCR_MAX_SIDE`). Never shrink a normal picture (the old reader cut phone screenshots to 1600 px and misread them) and do not recolour it (greyscale and invert read worse and lose coloured rows): measure on real screenshots before adding any step. A row with a coloured band across it (the player's own, say) vanishes in the whole-page read, so for every place that read skipped (`League.gaps`) just that stretch is read again alone. `League.scan()` is the one reading of a scan: it feeds your own row in the form and the league table, flags every row that does not add up, prefers the printed points over the record (special events score differently), never invents a figure, and never takes "Cristhian M…" for the user. Nothing is saved without the person seeing the doubtful rows, and a player the scan missed can be added by hand. Known limits: a name with a number at its end ("Player 7") loses it, and a misread name on the first scan of a season cannot be told from a new player.
- One night can come in several pictures (the table did not fit one screen), and the day must count once: `#ocr-file` takes them all (and `#ocr-more` adds more to a table already read), `Arena.readPictures` reads each on its own and joins them with `League.extend(rows, text, known)`, so they are ONE table. `extend` keeps the rows already there (edits and warnings included), never adds a player twice (same name, or same place with a misread or cut-off name), never adds points up, fills a figure one picture lacks from the other, flags `conflict` where two pictures give a player different points (the first stays) and `place` where two players claim one place, and orders by place, so a second half that starts at place 18 is not "a table with a hole in it" (`League.gaps` skips a stretch too short for the places above it). A date written over a picture ("July 16") is not a player: `parseLine` drops a single word that has no record and no place with points. A table for a day that already has a saved tournament is joined to it with `League.mergeStored` ("Sumar al torneo guardado", undoable), and saving a table for a day that already has one asks first (`Arena.sameDayTournament`): saved apart, that night would be counted twice in the league.
- How many qualify differs from one season to the next: `State.seasonTop` (`{ "2026-2": 10 }`, key `pauper_arena_season_top`, its own test-mode key, in backups as `seasonTop`, cleaned by `cleanSeasonTop`) is asked in the season dialog together with the closing date, and `State.topFor(season)` gives the usual `CONFIG.LEAGUE.TOP_CUTOFF` until a season says. Never read the top from `CONFIG` directly.
- The General tab says who is already settled and how likely the rest is. `Outlook.decided()` is arithmetic, not a guess: someone is "in" when nobody can put them outside the top and "out" when they cannot get in by taking every point still on offer (it also counts a player who has not shown up yet, so an early "in" is rare on purpose). Both groups are green with a word on them, because the user asked for that. `Outlook.risk()` is a seeded simulation of the league nights left (an estimate, labelled so; exact when someone is decided). Neither speaks unless the season has a closing date, the league's weekday is known and every night played has a saved table; the nights left come from `Arena.nightsLeft()` (the nights on `State.leagueDay()`, today's included until its table is saved).
- A saved tournament may carry `playerResults` (everyone on the scanned standings screen: `{ name, pts, wl?, reto? }`). The General tab adds them up per season with `League.standings()`. It is an optional field, so the backup format stays `pauper-deck-arena-v1` (and `profile` is an optional field of the backup the same way: an older copy without it leaves the name and league day that are set); read it only through `League.cleanRows()`, because a backup file can put anything there.
- The store also hands out a table for each month with extra "reto" (side-quest) points: per night, the night's points and the reto next to them, then the total. The card "Tabla del mes de la tienda (retos)" on the General tab (`arena-monthly.js`; the reading and comparing is `Monthly` in `monthly.js`, pure, no DOM) reads that picture and sets it against the nights the person saved. A reto is the optional `reto` (a whole number, 1 or more) on that player's row of that night's `playerResults`, and `League.standings()` counts `pts + reto` per night (`retoPts` is the part that came from retos), so a total on the General tab is the store's total. The poster is coloured art behind white digits, so one read misses cells: `readMonthlyPicture` reads each picture four times (as it is, and three times through a "whiteness" filter, `whiteCanvas`, that turns the white digits black on white; one of them enlarged), then, with the rows located (`Monthly.frame`), `readMonthCrops` reads each row's name area (three settings) and figures (one) on their own, and `Monthly.parse` merges all the reads by position and takes every cell by vote. The crops matter most for names: the whole-page read runs the two lines of a name into one word on a busy picture, and on a real month (31 players, three pictures) it left 5 names wrong with no flag where the crops got all 31 right, so when crop reads of the names exist (`nameOnly`) only they vote for names. Measure on real pictures before touching any of this (all four whole-page reads stay: dropping one costs little, dropping two does not). The one thing the poster guarantees, that on every row the nights and the retos add up to the total, judges the result: it picks between two readings of a cell, and derives a single missing figure only when every other cell of the row was seen by two reads (and says so, `derived`). A row that is not sure is shown under "Filas por revisar" with its reason in words and does not count (not in the comparison, not in any save) until the person fixes it or says "Está bien así" (`Arena.confirmMonthRow`): figures missing or not adding up, a figure nobody read that the sum supplied (`derived`), a reading chosen because it makes the row add up (`fixed`), a name one letter off a player already saved (`snapped`, a guess; the same letters with the spaces lost is not) or read with little confidence (`name`). Each figure remembers how it was got (`row.how`), so typing over one figure, or the name, settles only that. When the columns cannot be told apart, or the header has more dates than there are night columns, it says so instead of returning a plausible table. Keep that bar (nothing silent, never a guessed figure) and measure on a real poster before adding a step. The date over each column says day and month only: `Monthly.isoFor` takes the latest year that is not in the future (a night saved in another year does not decide it), the person can always correct it, and two columns on one date are not compared at all (`clash`). `Monthly.compare` reads the person's rows through `League.cleanRowsAt` (as the league does: cleaned, with where each sits in what is stored, for writing back) and sets every player of the poster against the night saved for that date (`same`, `differs`, `absent`, `rest` when the store gave nothing and the player was not there, `unsure` when a name fits two rows; players are matched by `League.matchPlayer`: the exact name first, then one letter off on a long name, then a cut-off name) and adds up each player's month to set against the store's total. A month's table often comes in several pictures (10 or 11 players each: one month was 3 pictures, another 2, another 4): `Arena.readMonthFiles` reads every chosen picture with one engine, each on its own layout (pages differ by a few pixels), and `Monthly.join` makes them ONE table once `Monthly.order` has put the pages in the store's order (best player first), whatever order the files were chosen in. A player on two pictures counts once, and who is the same player is decided carefully because a wrong merge makes a real player vanish: only players of EARLIER pictures can match (never one of the same picture), a held row merges with at most one incoming row (an exact name beats a close one; a tie merges nothing), and a row the person has touched (`row.touched`) or that is already in dispute is never overwritten. Two readings of one player that differ and both add up are a `conflict` row, which only the person's "Está bien así" settles (typing a figure does not), and a row shows every doubt it carries at once so that yes never clears one unseen. Each night's day is the vote of the pictures (a table that stands for several counts for them all; a header one picture cannot read, another does); another number of nights, another month, or days that disagree on several nights is refused, and so is a picture that is no table (it is named, and nothing is applied: all or nothing, so a page of players is never quietly missing). `Arena.dropMonth()` (test league, wipe, import) drops the table held and any reading under way, which would otherwise be applied to the other data when it finished. "Agregar otra captura de esta tabla" (`#month-more-file`) joins more pictures to the table held, keeping what the person corrected (`table.pictures`, `row.from` say which picture a row is on). The page must do all of this by itself (the person has nobody to ask, so nothing here may depend on a developer running a check or fixing data): that includes giving the person the nights they never saved. A night the poster has and the person lacks is made from the store's table: `Monthly.plan` finds them (a date with no tournament at all is made; a tournament the person saved without a table receives the store's table, and their own points for it follow the store's), `Monthly.create` does it, `Arena.makeMonthNights` asks first and can be undone ("Crear las noches que faltan con la tabla de la tienda"; the card is always on the General tab for this reason). A made night is an entry in `State.historic` with `deck: 'No participó'`, `source: 'store'`, `type: 'none'` and the table in `playerResults`: nobody's tournament, so the deck figures, attendance and every "No participó" check ignore it for free, and backup, undo, wipe, the player list and deleting from the Historial (where it reads "Noche de la liga") all work with no plumbing. In storage it is the one exception to "in `State.historic`": `State.save` writes the made nights under their own key (`State.storeNightsKey()`, `pauper_arena_store_nights`, with its test-mode key, cleared by `wipeAll` and `clearTestData`) and `State.load` joins them back, and a backup carries them in the optional field `storeNights`, never among its `historic` tournaments (`countEntries` words the two apart). The reason is rolling back: Vercel can put the previous release back in seconds, and that release reads only the history key and a backup's `historic`, so it would list these nights as "No participó" tournaments and count them. Rule for anything new the page stores: the history key and a backup's `historic` hold only what the release before understands; anything new goes under a key or field of its own, which an older release ignores. It was checked by running the live release on data this release had saved (all four tabs identical to its own data), in both directions for backups, for the service worker and for the `#tab=league` address. `League.standings` counts such a night only where the person has no table of their own for that day (never twice; a tournament of theirs without a table is "covered" by it), and reports `stored`, `nights` and, for the person, `storePts`/`storeNights` (their points on days with no tournament of theirs) which "Tus puntos" adds. `Monthly.entryFor` picks a day's night: the person's own with a table, then the store's, then their own without. When the header's days cannot be read, `Monthly.guessDays` proposes the league weekday's days of that month (only when there are as many as the columns and none contradicts a day that was read); the fields say "(propuesta)" until the person sets them. Keep these rules (each came from a review): `isStoreNight` (`format.js`) is the ONE test for "made from the store's table" (both `source: 'store'` and the "No participó" deck, never `source` alone); when the person later saves their own table for a day that has such a night (the Registrar, or "Sumar al torneo guardado"), `League.absorb` brings the store's retos and the players their scan missed into it, keeps their own points, and removes the store's night (undoable), so nothing is lost; a night already made from the same table on another date (the poster read again with a date corrected) is moved to the right date (`plan.move`), never made twice (the night moved has exactly this table's players, points and retos, is not on a date another column of the poster stands on, lies within a month of the new date and is taken by one column only); a day is its real date, so `State.fillIsoDates` repairs dates on load and on import (only a date that can really be read becomes one: it never invents one, because the date parser falls back to today); the risk estimate counts `table.nights` (every night added up), not `captured` (the person's own played nights that are covered); the league weekday is inferred from the person's own tournaments only; the points chart and the history count leave these nights out. Two actions, both explicit and undoable: "Guardar los retos" writes only the retos, and "Usar los puntos de la tienda donde no coinciden" (asks first) also replaces the night points that differ and adds the players the person's table lacks (its dialog names who would come in: a player saved under another spelling would be counted twice). A night where most players differ (`Monthly.mismatched`, more likely the wrong date than many misreadings) makes either action ask "¿Es la fecha correcta?" first. The table held in `Arena._month` is dropped when the data under it changes (test mode, wipe, import), and reading another picture asks before replacing one the person has corrected. The poster lists only the top 10, so the others cannot be checked, and a night with no saved table cannot be compared. Known limit: a poster read through heavy noise is flagged or refused, never trusted. Accessibility rules of this card: the buttons under the table are drawn again with it, so after an action focus is put back with `focusMonthAction` (a redraw used to leave it on `body`); `#month-status` says each reading step once (never once per percent: it is a live region), the hidden `#month-announce` says what the comparison now says only when that changes, and a toast says the table was read; every editor field names its night ("13 ago: reto"), and a reto or an unread figure in the table is named in hidden text, because size and colour say nothing to a screen reader.
- What the user kept from before this: their 2026-1 league, name, league day and 2026-2 closing date were in the code until October 2026 and now live in a private backup file they hold (`Documents/Pauper Deck Arena/liga-2026-1.json`, not in the repo). Their browser keeps what it saved; "Importar" replaces everything, so that file is only for an empty browser, and a fresh export from the live app is the real copy. The git history still holds the old `js/data.js`.
- Every JS file starts with a one-line description, a `/* global */` list of the names it takes from other files, and a `/* exported */` list of what it shares. Keep those lists current when you move or add a name.
- `sw.js`: precaches every file listed in `SHELL` and fetches HTML, css, js and the manifest network-first, so a new page never runs old scripts. When you add, rename or remove a css or js file, update `SHELL` and bump `CACHE_NAME`.
- Hosted on Vercel as a static site (`vercel.json` only sets headers). `main` is production and is updated by pull request from `testing_branch`.

## CSS formatting (this project's exception to the Code Guide)

The global Code Guide says one declaration per line. In this project a rule may sit on one line when it has up to 3 declarations and the line is at most 100 characters (for example `.btn-primary:hover { background-color: var(--color-brand-hover); }`). Anything longer, with more declarations, or with a comment on a declaration stays one declaration per line. Everything else in the Code Guide still applies: declaration order, logical properties, modern colour syntax, one selector per line when grouped.

## Design standard: tasteskill v2 house rules

Apply these rules to all CSS and UI work. They override any default AI tendencies.

### Hard bans — never write these

| Banned | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| `linear-gradient` or `radial-gradient` as backgrounds, on buttons, or in text | Flat color |
| `backdrop-filter: blur(...)` — glass panels | Solid opaque background |
| `box-shadow` on elements at rest (cards, buttons, icons) | Tone step between surfaces (`background` color difference) |
| `text-shadow` glows | Color alone for emphasis |
| `background: linear-gradient` on nav active states | Flat `rgba` tint |
| Ambient gradient blobs (`body::before/::after` with radial gradients) | Nothing — the background color is the background |
| Gradient on `<button>` or `.btn` | Single flat fill color; darker shade on `:active` |
| `:focus` box-shadow glow ring wider than 2px | 2px offset ring in the accent color, no spread |

### Shadows — when they ARE allowed

Shadows are only for elements that truly float above the page:
- Dropdown menus, popovers, modals, dialogs

Everything else uses a background tone step to separate surfaces.

### Color system

One ground, one ink, one accent. The current system:
- Ground: `#0a0e14` (`--bg-main`)
- Surfaces: `#111822` → `#16202e` → `#1c293c` (three tone steps, no lines needed between them)
- Ink: `#f0f6fc` (`--text-primary`)
- Accent: `#f97316` (`--color-brand`) — appears on primary action and one other thing max
- Status colors: green `#10b981`, cyan `#06b6d4`, red `#f43f5e` — semantic use only

Do not introduce violet, indigo, yellow-cream, or terracotta accents.

### Typography

Two faces max: Outfit (display/headings) and Inter (body). Already loaded.
- Running text: 16px min, `--text-primary` color, never soft grey for primary content
- Labels and secondary info: `--text-secondary` or `--text-muted`, sentence case preferred
- Avoid tiny all-caps tracked labels in marketing contexts; acceptable for table headers and form labels in this app context

### Buttons

Flat fill, one color. Square corners (`var(--radius-sm)` = 8px) already set.
- Primary: `background: var(--color-brand)`, dark text
- Active state: darker flat shade, no shadow
- No gradients, no glows

### Motion

Keep existing transitions (opacity, transform only). No scroll event listeners for animation — use `IntersectionObserver` or CSS `@keyframes`. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### What's exempt (per tasteskill spec)

- Real `<table>` borders and form field borders are fine
- Focus rings on interactive elements are required for accessibility
- `<pre>`/`<code>` blocks may use monospace
